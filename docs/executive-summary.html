Geological hypothesis

The r13 lattice (206,895 dots) is a regular grid whose 3-px halo covers 99.87% of the template footprint. The ~6,514 footprint pixels outside that halo — and outside known catalogue faults — are locations the lattice systematically misses. Radiometric-scarp coincidence in these gaps marks concealed fault conduits absent from both the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue and the lattice grid.

  • Layers: GeoDAWN radiometric (K/Th/U/TC), LiDAR scarp features, USGS SGMC mapped faults, catalogue labels, r13 lattice exclusion grid.
  • Physical signature: point thermal/scarp coincidence (concealed conduit emergence), not proximity to known faults.
  • Why it catches missing faults: hot-spring/radiometric anomalies in lattice gaps are surface expressions of conduits neither the catalogue nor a regular grid samples.
  • Non-fault mimic: alluvial-fan aprons, playa-mud cracks, drainage-channel margins.
  • Differs from existing submissions: every existing focus-site winner dots catalogue-proximity or dense-lattice locations; this dots only lattice-gap geothermal anomalies.
