Why GEMSDOE32 B2 reportedly led the focus-site list

The strongest explanation supported by the local forensic record is selective removal of redundant dots near mapped-fault traces, not evidence that a new geothermal vent detector was validated. The supplied B2 result is recorded as 37,654 dots. The prior local mask analysis describes B2 as the D2.8 H19-5 dotted mask restricted to pixels more than two 100-m pixels from the known-fault catalogue. D1.5, D2.8, R1, B2, H33D and the GEMSDOE36 PINN variant form a controlled historical family: progressively thinning/altering a dense ridge mask changed the false-positive burden and yielded the supplied sequence of owner-reported scores.

The metric is a distance-weighted Tversky score with false-positive weight alpha=0.2, false-negative weight beta=0.8, and a 300-m triangular support kernel, as implemented in src/gems25/metric.py. Known mapped faults are masked in the competition's target per the repository's archived staff-clarification citation. That makes overlap with mapped traces a poor way to add true-positive credit; removing low-value points can reduce the denominator. This mechanism is plausible and consistent with the reported ordering, but the actual hidden positive map is unavailable, the organizer receipt was not supplied, and exact leaderboard scoring cannot be reconstructed from public training labels. Thus this is a mechanistic explanation, not proof of why the organizer score was exactly 0.2778.

Could a new submission exceed the owner-reported 0.2778? Possible in principle, not demonstrated. This turn's CBEC spatial-block HOLDOUT-DTI was below its same-run H35 control and did not pass the frozen gate. The earlier H78 0.2785 was an unsupported code constant, not a measured leaderboard prediction. No higher-score claim is justified.
