### Why B2 plausibly outperformed its listed neighbours

Computed from pinned owner-mirror rasters, the masks form a sequence of denser-to-sparser predictions: GEMSDOE25 D2.8 equals GEMSDOE30 D2.8; GEMSDOE28 R1 equals GEMSDOE31; GEMSDOE32 B2 removes the additional catalogue-adjacent shell; and GEMSDOE36 retains B2 while adding 1,200 dots. The B2 mask contains 37,654 positive pixels and is the D2.8 mask with predictions at catalogue distance `<= 2 px` removed. This is a geometry result, not a recovered hidden-label measurement.

Under the metric form recorded in `src/gems25/metric.py`, a prediction can add little new triangular-kernel TP credit when a nearby prediction already covers a truth pixel, while still adding FP mass. Thus removal of low-marginal-credit or redundant predictions can improve DTI without finding additional faults. Conditional on the supplied historical scores, B2's advantage is consistent with lowering denominator cost faster than useful credit. However, neither the public hidden labels nor an organizer receipt is available here; the score sequence does not prove that B2's deleted dots were false positives, nor that catalogue proximity is universally harmful. Reproducing B2 would be both an exact historical duplicate and an invalid lane choice.
