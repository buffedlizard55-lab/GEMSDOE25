# 07 · Findings (2026-10-02) — what the designed experiments, the forensics and the emission model say

Evidence classes: **COMPUTED** (script + JSON named), **OWNER** (owner-reported score, not a DrivenData receipt), **INFERENCE** (assumptions stated).
Pre-registered documents `04_*` and `05_*` were committed before the runs they govern; deviations are listed at the end.

## 1. The portal error — root cause (COMPUTED)
The first GEMSDOE25 "recommended" file invented its footprint: **2,344,929 of the 5,167,373 official footprint pixels were NaN**, 2,832,257 pixels outside it were finite,
and it emitted 1,249,834 positive pixels (`evidence/old_gems25_tif_forensics.json`). NaN fails every `0 ≤ x ≤ 1` test, which is exactly "Predicted values must be in
range [0, 1]". INFERENCE: the portal's validator is not public, so this is the data-supported cause, not a DrivenData confirmation. The new writer takes the footprint from the
organizers' template, refuses NaN/out-of-range inside it, and reproduces the portal-accepted 0.2477 file's pixels and raster profile exactly
(`tests/test_group_reproduction.py::test_writer_reproduces_the_portal_accepted_file_profile_and_pixels`).

## 2. Why 0.2477 scored best (COMPUTED + OWNER)
* The scored file is **pixel-for-pixel `dot_thin(H19-5, 1.5)`**: 60,069 of 121,131 pixels (49.6 %), zero NaN inside the footprint, none on a catalogue pixel (`evidence/why_0_2477.json`).
* Identity: `DTI = TP_w / (0.2 (TP_w + FP_w) + 0.8 |G|)`. A pixel pays for itself only if (extra credit)/(extra false-positive mass) > `0.2·DTI/(1−0.2·DTI)` ≈ **0.052** at 0.2477.
* Hidden-truth size from two independent live readings: blind lattice (owner 0.0904) → **12,503 px**; H19-5 → dotted pair → **12,769 px** (2 % apart). In the harness the lattice scores 0.0861 against 0.0904 live,
  so the density regime (20 % of catalogue hidden) matches.
* Geometric credit retention of the dotted file: 0.853 (truth assumed uniform near H19-5's detections). Calibrated model (`evidence/emission_model.json`): credit 0.509·|G|, false-positive mass 8.7·|G|.
* Out-of-sample check on the pair not used for calibration (solid H25 0.1280 → dotted H28 0.1839, OWNER): the model predicts **0.1672** (error −0.017; it under-predicts the dotting gain).

## 3. Can we score higher than 0.2477?  What would 0.3195 take?
* **Emission only (live-calibrated):** model optimum at min-distance 2.4 (44,090 px): **0.2553, band 0.2500–0.2613**. The harness agrees on direction: dotted d2.8 beats dotted d1.5 in 4/4 folds (+0.0042, t = 3.2, 3 d.f.).
  INFERENCE: given the out-of-sample error (−0.017) the +0.008 expectation is smaller than the model's own error. My judgment is that D2.8 > D1.5 is more likely than not (roughly 60–70 %), not certain.
* **0.3195 requires more credit per emitted pixel, not more pixels:** with 60 k dots the file needs 0.568·|G| of credit (the 0.2477 file has 0.434), i.e. **+31 %**; with 40 k dots 1.63× the credit per pixel; with 30 k dots 1.94×.
  The rank-#5 score (0.2941) needs +20 % at 60 k dots. Nothing in this repository demonstrates such a detector.
* Score-ordered dotting (H26-0) beats score-blind `dot_thin` at equal pixel count on a continuous surface (+0.0037, 4/4 folds, t = 8.5) — usable for any *new* surface, not for the binary H19-5.

## 4. Fractional factorial 2^(5−1), Resolution V (COMPUTED, `evidence/factorial/results.md`)
16 runs × 8 hide-and-recover cells, response = exact sparse-regime DTI after a fixed emission. Lenth ME = 0.0139.

| Factor | Family | Main effect | Folds + | Class (frozen rule) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E | catalogue geometry (visible faults only) | **+0.0610** | 4/4 | matters alone |
| B | DEM curvature / scarp (100 m + 3DEP LiDAR descriptors) | **+0.0253** | 4/4 | matters alone |
| D | thermal / geochemical (radiometrics, conductivity, GDR springs & wells) | +0.0044 | 4/4 | inert (below ME; small but consistent) |
| A | potential-field gradients | −0.0027 | 1/4 | inert |
| C | strain / seismicity | −0.0109 | 0/4 | inert by rule (consistently negative, just under −ME) |

* **Sparsity of effects holds:** E and B carry **94 %** of the effect energy (E 80 %, B 14 %); the largest of the ten interactions is |−0.0050| (BC), below the ME. **No family "matters only in combination"** by the frozen rule.
* B×E = −0.0043: the two active families partly substitute (both encode fault-prone terrain). The model-predicted best corner, **B+E alone (0.145)**, lies in the unexecuted half-fraction and is tested in the confirmation replicate.
* AUC effects agree (E +0.204, B +0.065, BE −0.037). Hug-share (emission within 3 px of visible faults) is *lower* with E (−0.034): E does not make the emission cling to known faults.
* Same-harness references: random-score ridge/dots **0.066**, unsupervised topographic ridge 0.034, unsupervised potential-field ridge 0.040. Supervised surfaces with E/B reach 0.11–0.14. A 100 m tree model on potential-field, strain or earthquake bands carries **no transferable signal across quadrants** (A alone AUC 0.50, C alone 0.49).

**What this does not say.** Potential fields are not useless for faults: at 100 m, with generic trees and blocked validation they did not transfer, and concealed faults need dedicated processing (H26-3). Family E very likely flatters the proxy
(faults cluster; hidden and visible components are neighbours); the live record (≥45 % catalogue-adjacent emission scored ≤ 0.046) says proximity alone fails live.
