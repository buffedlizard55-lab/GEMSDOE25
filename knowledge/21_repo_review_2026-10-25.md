### Why the reported 0.2778 is plausible, and what is not established

The owner-mirror pixels support a clear **mask-edit** explanation: B2 removes a low-credit band of predictions close to already mapped faults from the D2.8 emission. If those predictions are false positives under the hidden test, deleting them reduces the false-positive denominator while preserving more useful credit. That can raise a Tversky/DTI score without improving fault detection. The inference is plausible, not proof of the hidden-label mechanism or the competition score.

The shared hide-and-recover proxy cannot validate the B2 premise: its hidden positives are withheld catalogue segments, so it tends to reward predictions near catalogue faults. The proxy’s target and the competition’s uncatalogued expert labels are not interchangeable. Therefore this review cannot claim that a new file beats 0.2778, 0.3195, 0.4413, or any other supplied leaderboard number. Conflicting current-best claims remain an open provenance issue (IR-25-LEADERBOARD-CONFLICT / IR-25-FOCUS-SCORES-2026-10-25).
