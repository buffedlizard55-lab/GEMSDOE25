## Why GEMSDOE32 B2 reportedly led the focus-site list

The strongest explanation supported by the local forensic record is **selective removal of redundant dots near mapped-fault traces**, not evidence that a new geothermal vent detector was validated. The supplied B2 result is recorded as 37,654 dots. The prior local mask analysis describes B2 as the D2.8 H19-5 dotted mask restricted to pixels more than two 100-m pixels from the known-fault catalogue. D1.5, D2.8, R1, B2, H33D and the GEMSDOE36 PINN variant form a controlled historical family: progressively thinning/altering a dense ridge mask changed the false-positive burden and yielded the supplied sequence of owner-reported scores.

The metric is a distance-weighted Tversky score with false-positive weight `alpha=0.2`, false-negative weight `beta=0.8`, and a 300-m triangular support kernel, as implemented in `src/gems25/metric.py`. Known mapped faults are masked in the competition's target per the repository's archived staff-clarification citation. That makes overlap with mapped traces a poor way to add true-positive credit; removing low-value points can reduce the denominator. This mechanism is plausible and consistent with the reported ordering, but the actual hidden positive map is unavailable, the organizer receipt was not supplied, and exact leaderboard scoring cannot be reconstructed from public training labels. Thus this is a **mechanistic explanation, not proof of why the organizer score was exactly 0.2778**.

### Metric mechanism and limits of the 0.2778 explanation

The shared metric source defines `TP_w = Σ_g max_x p(x) k(d(x,g))`, `FP_w = Σ_x p(x)[1−max_g k(d(x,g))]`, and `FN_w = Σ_g[1−max_x p(x)k(d(x,g))]`, with `k(d)=max(1−d/3,0)` at 100-m pixels. With `α=0.2`, `β=0.8` and `TP_w+FN_w=|G|`, the identity is `DTI = TP_w / (0.2 × (TP_w + FP_w) + 0.8 × |G| + ε)`. It rewards proximity, not exact pixels alone. An added dot is useful only if its marginal credit outweighs its marginal false-positive mass; redundant dots can lower the score even when they are physically plausible. Conversely, deleting dots increases missed-truth cost, heavily weighted by beta. This makes selective thinning a plausible tradeoff, not a guaranteed gain.

The pinned mask audit gives **COMPUTED GEOMETRY, not a score**: the D2.8 mask has 44,090 positive pixels; B2 removes 6,436 pixels at catalogue distance ≤200 m, leaving 37,654. GEMSDOE36 contains the B2 mask plus 1,200 pixels. The supplied scores (`0.2600` for D2.8, `0.2778` for B2, `0.2750` for GEMSDOE36) are each **OWNER-REPORTED**, not organizer-confirmed; their ordering is directionally consistent with low-value extra dots, but submissions may also differ in provenance, masking, grid encoding or evaluation conditions. No saved blind labels or receipt permit attribution of the 0.0178 reported difference to collar pruning alone. Selection across multiple submissions on a hidden leaderboard is itself a source of adaptive overfitting.

The study has an important boundary condition: competition masking removes the *known* catalogue pixels exactly, but the two-pixel collar is a modeling choice about near-fault areas, not a physically proven exclusion zone. A nearby blind fault can be real; pruning can also discard a true hidden target. The blocked hide-and-recover design in this turn tests CBEC on public catalogue gaps only; it does not retroactively validate B2 or recover the private competition truth.

Could a new submission exceed the owner-reported 0.2778? **Possible in principle, not demonstrated.** This turn's CBEC spatial-block HOLDOUT-DTI was below its same-run H35 control and did not pass the frozen gate. The earlier H78 `0.2785` was an unsupported code constant, not a measured leaderboard prediction. No higher-score claim is justified.
