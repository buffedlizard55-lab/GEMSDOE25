## 1. Why GEMSDOE32 `h33-2-b2` scored 0.2778 (OWNER-REPORTED score; anatomy COMPUTED)

1. **B2 is a pure subtraction file.** Pixel-for-pixel, B2 equals GEMSDOE28 `h27-4` minus every dot within 2 px of the catalogue
   (XOR = 0). `h27-4` equals the D2.8 dotting of H19-5 minus dots within 1 px. The file has 37,654 binary dots, nearest-neighbour spacing 3.0 px,
   and a median distance of 19.6 px to the catalogue (`evidence/top_scorer_forensics.json`).
2. **Every file scoring ≥ 0.2449 except GEMSDOE70 is a ≥ 96.9 % pixel subset of H19-5** (GEMSDOE19, 0.1922). GEMSDOE70 (0.2478) is the
   exception: only 14.3 % of its dots lie on H19-5 pixels, but 62 % lie within 3 px. Forensics now cover all 21 pinned rasters. The group's progress from 0.1922 to 0.2778 came from *emission*
   (dotting to 2.4–3 px and removing low-yield dots), not from new detection.
3. **Why removing dots helped (INFERENCE).** Under the official metric, known catalogue pixels are masked (OFFICIAL rules, docs.nlr.gov 96647;
   staff forum post linked on the site). Removing dots ≤1 px and then ≤2 px from the catalogue raised the score monotonically:
   0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778. So hidden truth is depleted within ≈200 m of mapped traces. Removing low-yield dots cuts FP faster than TP.
4. **Swapping failed for everyone.** Seven sibling files (GEMSDOE49/63/70/73/76/82/91) moved 34–49 % of their dots off the H19-5 halo,
   and all scored lower. Solving the DTI identity with B2 as anchor (`evidence/live_anchor_complement.json`, INFERENCE) gives the
   off-incumbent dot efficiency e (credit per dot relative to B2's average dot):

   | File | Owner-reported | Share near H19-5 | e |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | g70 dual-submod | 0.2478 | 0.620 | 0.71–0.72 |
   | g49 gate-ortho | 0.2376 | 0.512 | 0.63–0.65 |
   | g76 tiltworm | 0.2343 | 0.661 | 0.47–0.49 |
   | g73 mcsc | 0.1917 | 0.580 | 0.25–0.26 |
   | g91 lftg | 0.1823 | 0.557 | 0.21–0.22 |
   | g63 cvarlw | 0.2303 | 0.596 | 0.16–0.24 |
   | g82 punoisyor | 0.1886 | 0.613 | 0.15–0.17 |

   The live break-even for an *added* dot is e ≈ 0.33–0.47 (knowledge/17 §1). Three of seven detectors reached that level on
   their off-incumbent dots, but they **replaced** B2-quality dots (e = 1) instead of adding to them, so every swap lost.
   **Nobody had tried adding.** That observation became hypothesis G25-K1.

**Verdicts:** G25-K1 **negative**. The frozen gate (knowledge/17 §2) needs G1 = paired mean > +0.001, ≥ 3/4 folds positive and worst fold ≥ −0.01,
plus G2 = pooled R ≥ 0.50 with R ≥ 0.50 in ≥ 3/4 folds. K_self failed G1 on all three counts (−0.0007, 2/4, worst −0.0114). Its pooled R of 0.505
cleared 0.50, but it reached 0.50 in only 2/4 folds, so it also failed G2. G3 (K_rand R 0.183 < 0.50) and G4 (canary) passed. G25-K5 **negative**
(−0.0026, 2/4, worst −0.0125, R 0.470). No confirmation stage was run,
per the pre-registration. Two of the three budgeted experiments were used, and neither was tuned post hoc.

What was learned:
* **Fault-zone multiplicity is real but already captured.** Random dots in the 300–800 m flank band are 2× as efficient as random dots
  elsewhere (R 0.37 vs 0.18), which is consistent with distributed damage zones and fault-zone width (Wesnousky 2005,
  doi:10.1029/2004TC001645). The HGB ranking is better still over the whole zone (0.505), so restricting to the flank gains nothing.
* **The north–south split is the dominant uncertainty.** In the northern folds R ≈ 0.36 (below live break-even); in the southern folds R ≈ 0.6 (above it).
* **The holdout gate is structurally stricter than the live criterion.** At holdout DTI 0.15, G1 implies R > 0.57, whereas the live R* is ≤ 0.47.
  This is recorded as a design note. It is not grounds to relabel the result.
* **GEM-1 is random-level as a complement** (IR-25-GEM1-WEAK-CONTROL). Its PR #12 "validation" used a weak control.

## 4. Answer to the owner's question

*Why 0.2778?* B2 is the best emission of a strong but shared detector (H19-5 multiline corroboration). Its gains came from removing low-yield
dots near mapped faults, where the hidden truth is depleted. *Can we beat it?* Not on the evidence this session produced. Every unique file
built from the shared 100 m feature stack has to put its new dots where other sites have not already placed theirs, and that is lower-value
terrain. Beating 0.2778 (and the owner-reported 0.3195 / 0.4413 claims, which conflict: IR-25-LEADERBOARD-CONFLICT) needs a **genuinely new
detector**, not a new emission of the old one.
