## Why the reported GEMSDOE32 0.2778 is plausible — and what it does not establish

The local pixel identity audit says B2 is D2.8 thinned H19-5 with predictions at catalogue distance `d_cat <= 2 px` removed. Relative to the 44,090-pixel D2.8 file, that removes 6,436 pixels and leaves 37,654. The local interpretation is a *geometry/emission* change, not a newly detected set of faults. Under the DTI form recorded in the repo, each emitted pixel pays false-positive mass, while nearby predictions have overlapping 300 m support and truth credit uses a maximum within the triangular kernel. Thus thinning can discard redundant dots while retaining much of the same broad spatial coverage, and collar pruning can remove predictions with low marginal credit. This explains why B2 is a plausible improvement over its parent **conditional on the score claims**.

It does **not** prove that this mechanism caused the reported 0.2778: the hidden evaluation labels are not available to this audit, and the score is OWNER-REPORTED. The local work verifies raster identities and metric arithmetic, not the organizer's realized hidden truth. A candidate beating 0.2778 would need genuinely better credit density at useful fault locations, not merely another thinning/collar operation or a new name. The current component-withheld proxy can favor nearby catalogue remnants; H73-PM restored more far-field-like emission geometry but then lost to same-run H35. The next necessary scientific instrument in the prior handoff is a pre-registered block-withheld selector. There is no defensible conversion from its eventual HOLDOUT-DTI to a leaderboard-score projection yet.
