## 1. Why `h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros` scored 0.2778 (OWNER-reported; decomposition COMPUTED)

The competition metric is a distance-weighted Tversky-style index. For a binary dotted prediction the
organizer's pooled form collapses (COMPUTED algebra, forum-confirmed Tversky α = 0.2, β = 0.8, 300 m ≈ 3 px
kernel) to

$$ \mathrm{DTI} = \frac{\mathrm{TP}}{0.2\,(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}) + 0.8\,N}, $$

where TP is the *kernel-smeared, capped* credit collected against unseen target pixels. Two consequences
drive the whole leaderboard:

1. **Credit saturates, cost does not.** A dot within 3 px of a target pixel collects credit for that pixel;
   a second dot near the *same* target pixel collects ≈ 0 additional credit but still costs ≈ 1 FP at
   weight 0.2. Dotting (spacing emitted pixels so their 3 px discs do not double-claim the same targets)
   is therefore pure emission economics: it converts redundant FP into either savings or fresh coverage.
2. **Pixels on/near the known catalogue earn ≈ 0.** The target set is *unmapped* faults; catalogue-adjacent
   dots mostly buy FP. Deleting a collar around the catalogue removes near-worthless FP at almost no TP cost.

The pixel-exact nested chain (each file a strict subset of the previous; subset identities and distances
verified locally, `evidence/focus_sites_forensics.json`, `evidence/b2_microvariants.json` — the latter
regenerated this session and reproducing the pre-registration exactly) makes the mechanism measurable
step-by-step *from the owner's own scores*, without needing the unknown N:

| step (strict subsets, same detector) | pixels | OWNER score |
|---|---:|---:|
| H19-5 undotted surface (GEMSDOE19 lineage) | 121,131 | 0.1922 |
| dot-thin d1.5 (GEMSDOE24 best) | 60,069 | 0.2477 |
| dot-thin d2.8 (D2.8; GEMSDOE25) | 44,090 | 0.2600 |
| + delete d(catalogue) ≤ 1 collar ("R1"; GEMSDOE28/31 bests) | 40,199 | 0.2708 |
| + delete d(catalogue) ≤ 2 collar ("B2"; **GEMSDOE32**) | 37,654 (sha c55bafc4…) | **0.2778** |
| B2 + 1,200 Anderson-PINN pixels (GEMSDOE36 best) | 38,854 | 0.2750 (worse) |

Each deletion *raised* the score, which proves the deleted pixels carried below-marginal credit. The marginal
calculus at the B2 operating point: DTI rises when a pixel is added only if its expected credit exceeds
τ ≈ 0.056–0.059 (COMPUTED from the DTI equation at B2's counts). The owner's live ledger around B2 is a
complete map of that threshold: 2 byte/content rehosts reproduce 0.2778; all 15 scored micro-variants
(add/delete shells, re-thins) land at 0.2720–0.2775, all below; the d = 3 shell (B2's next collar) carries
≈ 0.064 credit/dot > τ, so widening the moat to B = 3 is expected to *hurt*. Four independent-detector swaps
on the same emission recipe score 0.1816–0.2478 — the recipe is not the moat, the *detector surface* is.
0.2778 is thus best understood as: a good H19-5/H27-era ranked surface, thinned to near-optimal spacing,
with its two lowest-credit shells removed, sitting essentially at the emission optimum of that detector.
The focus-site bests are all the same lineage: DOE24 0.2477, DOE25 0.2600, DOE27 0.2449, DOE28 0.2710,
DOE30 0.2600, DOE31 0.2708, DOE32 0.2778, DOE33 0.2632, DOE36 0.2750 (all OWNER-reported;
`evidence/focus_sites.json`).

**What beating it requires.** Not emission tuning — that knob is demonstrably exhausted on this surface
(+0.01 DTI would need ≈ +4 % credit density at fixed emission). It requires a detector that re-ranks pixels
so that, at the same ~37–49 k emitted pixels, a larger share lands within 3 px of *unmapped* fault pixels.
That is a genuine geophysics problem: find the signature of faults that the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue missed —
typically because they have no scarp (buried beneath Quaternary alluvium/Lake Lahontan sediments), which is
precisely where every scarp/curvature detector in this repo's history is blind, and where the holdout proxy
(withheld *catalogued* segments, mostly scarp-bearing) is an imperfect guide (a standing limitation: the
proxy↔live rank correlation over the owner's history is ρ ≈ +0.33, not significant).

## 2. Five untried hypotheses, ranked (full text: knowledge/21 §3)

1. **XG1 cross-gradient alignment** (chosen; §3). Layers already in the stack; physics: one fault plane
   steps density (band 13 isostatic gravity), magnetization (band 2 RTP) and base depth (band 15), so the
   three gradient vectors are parallel even under cover. Untried here: prior products (`X3_gm`, `X3_gd`)
   multiply gradient *magnitudes* (orientation-blind); H34 slices a single field.
2. **CURV-PERSIST**: multi-scale (σ = 1, 2, 4, 8 px) curvature zero-crossing persistence of the residual
   magnetics (band 12) — a fault's flexure persists across scales; noise does not. Cheap; plausible next slot.
3. **SPRING-ALIGN**: alignment of emission with INGENIOUS well/spring clusters (`external/…wellspring…csv`)
   — hydrothermal upwelling rides permeable fault damage zones; not a detector feature so far.
4. **EQ-RIDGE**: ridge transform of earthquake-density bands 10/16 (lineations of microseismicity along
   active-but-unmapped structures). Weak prior (catalogue completeness).
5. **SHORELINE-SUPPRESS** (negative control): Lahontan shoreline terraces mimic fault scarps in DEM/LiDAR
   families; suppressing the fixed-elevation shoreline band could remove systematic FP. **BLOCKED**: needs
   absolute elevations; USGS 3DEP is free and official but is not fetchable inside this sandbox's network
   allowlist — recorded, not silently dropped.

## 3. XG1: design and result (all numbers HOLDOUT-DTI, evaluator `gems25.metric.dti_binary`, α .2 β .8 R 3 px)

Transform (`src/gems25/xgrad.py`, unit-tested): nearest-filled gravity/RTP/log1p(base-depth) fields;
plain σ = 1.5 px gradients; p99-normalised magnitudes; per field pair,
`ridge_strength(|cos ∠(∇a, ∇b)| × min(|∇a|', |∇b|'))` — three columns `XG_gm/gd/md_align_ridge`.
Named mimics: lithologic/intrusive contacts and caldera ring margins (parallel gradients at one boundary,
no fault), and common flight-line levelling artefacts in GeoDAWN-style aeromagnetics/gravity grids.
Method lineage: cross-gradient coupling of Gallardo & Meju (2003, *Geophys. Res. Lett.* 30:1658,
doi:10.1029/2003GL017370; 2004 *JGR* extension doi:10.1029/2003JB002716).
Data lineage: GeoDAWN airborne magnetic/radiometric surveys (Glen & Earney, USGS data release 2024,
doi:10.5066/P93LGLVQ, CC0 1.0); INGENIOUS geothermal play-fairway compilation (GDR submission 1391,
doi:10.15121/1881483) — both already the provenance of the pinned competition stack; **no new external
data were needed or fetched**.

Design (frozen before fitting): 4 spatially blocked folds (NW/NE/SW/SE, whole fault segments
withheld with buffer, catalogue features refit on visible faults only, visible faults masked
pixel-exactly), four additive paired conditions per cell at identical emission (score-ordered dots
2.4 px, 3.5 % budget): C = BDE+7, B35 = C + H27_tip + H34 (the current comparable same-run best),
X = B35 + XG1 (primary), Xonly = C + XG1 (diagnostic only, never promotable). Screen on fresh
draws 14–15; confirmation on 16–17 **only** after a screen pass. Gate (X vs B35): mean paired
gain > +0.001, ≥ 3/4 folds positive, worst fold ≥ −0.010, catalogue-hugging increase ≤ +0.10,
leakage canary max(AUC, 1−AUC) ≤ 0.90 per cell.

*Interpretation (INFERENCE).* At σ = 1.5 px (150 m), cross-gradient alignment of these three grids adds
no ranking information beyond what the magnitude/slice features already carry for *catalogue-like*
faults: either the alignment signal is dominated by lithologic contacts (the named mimic), or the
catalogue-missing fault population whose gradients would align is rare among withheld
catalogued segments (proxy blindness — untestable without a live slot we correctly refuse to spend).
No σ/band re-runs were attempted: that would be tuning on the outcome, which the pre-registration
forbids. Any retry needs a fresh pre-registration, fresh draws, and an a-priori reason to expect a
different scale/pairing to behave differently.
