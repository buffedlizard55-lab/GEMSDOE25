# D01 outcome and the executability question — 2026-10-26

**Bottom line: no new TIF is approved for submission, and none was produced.** Two results, both computed this session, explain why, and both are needed by whoever decides the next policy step. They are negative deliverables, not a pretext to bless an old file.

## 1. Why the reported 0.2778 can be high — established facts vs inference

The strongest supplied historical exemplar is GEMSDOE32 `h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros` at **0.2778, OWNER-REPORTED** (no organizer receipt has been supplied). Earlier fresh pixel checks ([`evidence/focus_audit_2026-10-26.json`](../evidence/focus_audit_2026-10-26.json)) established that B2 is exactly the D2.8 mask with 6,436 predictions within 200 m of the supplied catalogue removed. Because DTI scores only the strongest nearby prediction per truth pixel while charging false-positive mass (`S = T/(0.2(T+F)+0.8N)`), deleting predictions that buy little credit but carry false-positive mass raises the score exactly when `t/f < 0.2S/(1−0.2S)`. B2 is a *selective* edit of one specific region (the near-catalogue collar), not a uniform thinning. That is the mechanically plausible reason a 44,090-dot parent at 0.2600 and a 37,654-dot near-catalogue-pruned child at 0.2778 can differ. **The hidden labels are unavailable, so this remains a conditional explanation, not a proven cause.** A near-catalogue prediction may equally be a misregistered or splay fault that would have earned credit.

The supplied leaderboard values 0.4413 and 0.3195 conflict with each other and have no receipts here. They are not verified targets, and no projection in this repository is allowed to become one.

## 2. The literal lane rule caps a candidate at 21,713 unique dots

Preregistered before running ([`knowledge/29_lane_budget_preregistration_2026-10-26.md`](29_lane_budget_preregistration_2026-10-26.md); the shared parallel implementation is preregistered in [`knowledge/29_lane_feasibility_plan_2026-10-26.md`](29_lane_feasibility_plan_2026-10-26.md)), computed with the shared strict-lane tool `src/gems25/lane_feasibility.py` (this session first drafted a duplicate module and audit script; after merging `origin/main` that private fork was **deleted** in favour of the shared implementation) into [`evidence/lane_feasibility_2026-10-26.json`](../evidence/lane_feasibility_2026-10-26.json), plus an independent re-derivation of the same numbers through [`scripts/run_d01_density.py`](../scripts/run_d01_density.py):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Template footprint | 5,167,373 px (EPSG:32611, 3730 × 3292, 100 m) |
| Catalogue-positive pixels | 60,988 |
| Eligible candidate domain (footprint minus catalogue) | 5,106,385 px |
| Binding registry raster | `13gems_20261001_r13-lattice-s5_v2_nan-outside.tif`, SHA-256 `b4570b406efec198eb2f4d142ad8d0d6184b14b66f2f52b5c4d697f0647dc9b2` |
| Its 3-px halo coverage of the domain | 99.8724% |
| Pixels outside the halo (U) | 6,514 |
| Exact maximum feasible unique dots | 21,713 |

Derivation: with K unique dots, at most U can fall outside the halo, so the rule `in-halo/K ≤ 0.7` requires `10·max(0,K−U) ≤ 7·K`. The largest integer K satisfying it is `floor(10U/3) = 21,713`, and only a candidate that places a dot on **every** uncovered pixel reaches it. This is a *necessary* bound only: spacing, geology, rank correlation and the final-dot measurement still apply. It is reproduced by the shared brute-force tests in [`tests/test_lane_feasibility.py`](../tests/test_lane_feasibility.py) and by an independent integer check in this session's records.

The same arithmetic applied to candidate budgets: 38,854 → cannot pass; 44,090 → cannot pass; 59,472 → cannot pass; 70,524 → cannot pass. So a candidate at any historical scoring density cannot satisfy the literal rule against this raster.

## 3. D01: what lane-compatible density costs (HOLDOUT-DTI)

Preregistered in [`knowledge/30_preregistered_d01_density_2026-10-26.md`](30_preregistered_d01_density_2026-10-26.md), run once by [`scripts/run_d01_density.py`](../scripts/run_d01_density.py) into [`evidence/d01_density_2026-10-26/analysis.json`](../evidence/d01_density_2026-10-26/analysis.json). Incumbent H35 surface, no geology change, four buffered spatial quadrants, draws 60/61, one fit per cell shared by every density, spacing 2.4 px, all 67 feature canaries passed (maximum AUC 0.8025).

| Requested full-footprint cap | HOLDOUT-DTI | 95% CI (quadrant cluster bootstrap) | Median emitted per quadrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12,000 | 0.0639 | 0.0434–0.0943 | 960 |
| **21,713 (lane-compatible)** | **0.0859** | 0.0636–0.1145 | 1,677 |
| 26,494 | 0.0937 | 0.0722–0.1214 | 2,022 |
| 37,654 (B2-scale) | 0.1088 | 0.0867–0.1319 | 2,820 |
| 44,090 (D2.8-scale) | 0.1145 | 0.0929–0.1359 | 3,265 |
| 59,472 | 0.1257 | 0.1028–0.1464 | 4,280 |
| 70,524 (current request) | 0.1321 | 0.1104–0.1488 | 5,012 |

Paired difference at the lane cap versus the current request: **−0.0462**, 95% CI [−0.0558, −0.0321]; worse in every quadrant and both draws. Proxy DTI is monotone in emitted density across the tested range, so **uniform thinning toward the lane budget is not a scoring strategy**. That is consistent with the B2 story above: B2 is selective collar pruning, not uniform sparsification.

Caveat, recorded in [`run_card.json`](../evidence/d01_density_2026-10-26/run_card.json) as `IR-26-REQUESTED-CAP-SATURATION`: requested caps saturate at the available ridge candidates, so per-quadrant emitted counts are well below the caps. D01 orders density regimes; it does not simulate one exact full-grid file. A direct full-grid test would need an all-cell model and a full-surface emitter, outside this session's budget.

## 4. What this means for the deliverable

- The downloadable research TIF remains [`gems25-h71-hgpc-blind-b2-sapd3-k3p5-20261026-9eb0c31e5149-zeros.tif`](../docs/downloads/gems25-h71-hgpc-blind-b2-sapd3-k3p5-20261026-9eb0c31e5149-zeros.tif). It was revalidated against the template this session by `gems25.submission.check_file`: format-valid, 0 NaN inside the 5,167,373-px footprint, values in [0,1], 59,472 emitted pixels. It is a **2026-10-26** file, not a copy, but it **fails** its holdout gate and 100% of its dots sit inside the dense lattice halo. **DO NOT SUBMIT.**
- No new candidate was built, because there is currently no density at which a candidate can be both lane-clean and credibly competitive, and writing a knowingly inferior file would not be "the highest scoring submission".
- **A leaderboard score cannot be estimated here.** The private labels and organizer receipts are absent, and the proxy-versus-live rank correlation measured earlier (≈ +0.33, n.s., n = 24 by GEMSDOE24) is not a calibration. The executive table therefore states "no estimate available" instead of inventing a number. The only defensible quantitative claims are HOLDOUT-DTI with its CI and the owner-reported historical scores.

## 5. The decision that unblocks the project

`registry/irregularities.json` `IR-26-LANE-BUDGET-CONFLICT` records the conflict. One of two policies is needed, explicitly, by the owner:

1. **Exclude the synthetic sibling lattice from the lane registry** (keep reporting its overlap separately). Rationale: `13gems_20261001_r13-lattice-s5_v2_nan-outside.tif` is a deliberately regular lattice submission, so overlap with it is not evidence of drifting into another scientific lane. This restores the rule's original purpose. All duplicate flags already recorded stay as recorded.
2. **Adopt the symmetric density-aware statistic** already specified in `knowledge/22` for rasters whose own halo covers ≥70% of the footprint. Precedent exists in the repo; it was previously not applied unilaterally.

After either decision, the next run should be a full-grid density sweep with an all-cell fit, so the emission density can be chosen from evidence and the per-file HOLDOUT-DTI measured on an exact final dot set — then the surface and final-dot lane checks, the exact-file validator, and only then a separate selector decision.
