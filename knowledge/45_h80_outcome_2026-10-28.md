## 2. Why GEMSDOE32 B2 may have improved — and what cannot be inferred

The pixel identity is established on restored, pinned bytes. The hidden scoring labels and an organizer receipt are not available, so hidden-label causation is not established.

In the shared local DTI implementation, truth credit takes the maximum distance-weighted prediction around each truth pixel, whereas false-positive mass is summed across predictions. Sparse placement can therefore reduce redundant prediction mass without proportionately losing coverage. Pruning near-catalogue dots could improve that tradeoff; it can also hurt if new hidden faults actually occupy those locations. An exact copy of B2 is not a novel method.

For the local formula `S = T / (0.2(T + F) + 0.8N)`, deleting dots with lost credit `t` and removed false-positive mass `f > 0` improves S iff `t/f < 0.2S/(1 - 0.2S)`. This is a conditional algebraic statement, not a measurement of t/f on hidden truth. It does not establish a mathematical ceiling for B2, prove catalogue-shadow truth absence, or justify a forecast above the owner-reported 0.2778. The supplied overall-leader claims 0.3195 and 0.4413 remain conflicting, unverified context.
