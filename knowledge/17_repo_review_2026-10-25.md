## Findings and changes

### 1. Critical: lane “exception” contradicted the protocol

`evidence/gem_validation/results.json` recorded `0.8890153671` candidate-dot overlap within 3 px against the dense lattice (the tracked report does not confirm which candidate emitted those dots), and `evidence/gem_validation/lane_gem1.json` recorded a full-footprint GEM-1 overlap of `1.0`. Both exceed the literal `0.70` cutoff. After the review began, merged PR #13 added a GEM-FUSE1 TIFF and receipt that records `1.0` candidate-dot overlap with the r13 lattice. The earlier conclusion called this a “density artefact” and cleared it against selected “substantive” rasters. That is not a defensible exception: the user instructed that crossing the threshold means duplicate and stop.

**Correction:** the GEM-FUSE1 evidence and registry now preserve the measurement but record `DUPLICATE LANE / STOP`, `submission_allowed: false`, and `slot_approved: false`. Its primary and zeros fallback are testing-only; GEM-1 and H31-C remain un-cleared. The evidence record preserves the measured values rather than deleting the failure.

### 2. High: lane validation could be attached to the wrong candidate / coordinate grid

The prior validator checked one candidate's cropped holdout emission, then selected a separate candidate for packaging. Crop-local dot indexes were passed to a full-grid distance check, and the selected lane result was not explicitly tied to each exact full-footprint candidate. This could produce false clearance.

**Correction:** `scripts/validate_gem_candidate.py` now requires full-grid arrays, checks absolute Spearman magnitude, creates exact full-footprint emissions, runs the lane test per candidate, and fails closed for an empty or unreadable registry. Packaging eligibility requires that candidate's own holdout, leakage, and lane gates all pass. `scripts/check_lane_duplicates.py` also uses absolute rank correlation and will not call an empty registry unique.

### 3. High: executive summary mixed “do not submit” with upload instructions for that same file

The prior page marked the download not slot-approved but then gave direct steps to upload that exact candidate and referred to the wrong artifact's note/receipt values.

**Correction:** `scripts/build_site.py` now distinguishes an inspection download from an authorized submission. The current callout explicitly says “OK to download for inspection: YES” and “OK to submit: NO”; upload instructions are explicitly for a future selector-approved artifact only. The verification table and receipt now correspond to the featured artifact.

### 4. High: legacy packaging command could recreate a known duplicate

`scripts/package_submissions.py` unconditionally rebuilt the D2.8 TIFF already identified as byte-identical to a sibling submission, and could rewrite the registry with that file as primary. It was still listed in README's reproduction commands.

**Correction:** the legacy CLI now exits before reading inputs or writing files; its same-run receipt validator remains available and is unit-tested. README no longer suggests running the duplicate packager.

### 5. Data / reproducibility limit

Tests and lints were run from the pinned `requirements.txt`. The full spatial holdout, registry comparison, and exact-file validator were **not** rerun because required data and caches are absent. Stored receipts are historical owner-mirror evidence and do not establish organizer acceptance. `registry/run_card_repo_review_2026-10-25.json` records no current HOLDOUT-DTI, no current CI, and no new raster.

### 6. GEMSDOE32 `0.2778` interpretation

`knowledge/13_gemsdoe32_h33_score_review_2026-10-25.md` and `knowledge/14_gemsdoe32_analysis_and_hypotheses_2026-10-25.md` separate the owner’s `0.2778` claim, the sibling repository's documented deletion-only mechanism, and inference. The mechanism is plausible under the DTI cost/credit trade-off: removing predictions can improve the ratio if their expected credit is below their false-positive cost. However, the cited local `0.2747` is a projection, not the claimed `0.2778` score, and the catalogue-based hide-and-recover holdout cannot test whether hidden truth lies near or far from known faults. The score alone does not establish a new geological discovery or prove that the same pruning improves this project.
