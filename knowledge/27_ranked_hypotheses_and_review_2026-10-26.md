## Why B2 plausibly led this group (and what is not proven)

The published metric implementation in `src/gems25/metric.py` is a distance-tolerant Tversky score with `alpha=0.2`, `beta=0.8`, and a 300 m triangular kernel. For binary dots, a second nearby dot can add little or no new maximum credit to the same truth pixel while still adding false-positive mass. The measured local transformation chain is consistent with a high-utility operating point: H19-5 -> D1.5 -> D2.8 -> R1 -> B2 progressively reduces density and prunes catalogue-adjacent dots. The locally computed credit-per-deleted-dot diagnostics in `evidence/focus_sites_forensics.json` are conditional algebra reconstructed from the owner-supplied rounded scores and an assumed hidden-positive count; they are **not** direct measurements of hidden ground truth and must not be presented as HOLDOUT-DTI or ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED values.

Geophysically, the mask may remove detector responses that hug already mapped traces rather than identify omitted structures. That interpretation remains an **INFERENCE**. The benchmark labels and correction policy make the 0–300 m vicinity of known traces especially ambiguous; the catalogue-hide holdout cannot establish the live truth distribution for that region. The strongest path to improving over B2 is therefore a genuinely better, independently validated detector, not a score projection from another collar cut.
