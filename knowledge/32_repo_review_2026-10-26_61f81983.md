## Why GEMSDOE32 B2 is reported at 0.2778

The score classification comes first: **0.2778 is OWNER-REPORTED**, not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED. The local file geometry is reproducible; the score requires a receipt that is not present.

Pixel-exact forensics shows a nested pruning lineage:

- D2.8: 44,090 dots, reported 0.2600.
- R1: 40,199 dots, removing 3,891 D2.8 dots within 100 m of the visible catalogue; reported 0.2708.
- B2: 37,654 dots, removing another 2,545 dots in the 100–200 m shell; reported 0.2778.
- G36: B2 plus 1,200 dots; focus audit reports 0.2750, but the ledger's 0.2683 discrepancy remains unresolved.

This is consistent with the metric's mechanics: a prediction contributes distance-weighted coverage of hidden faults, while low-value extra predictions add false-positive mass. If the reported score anchors and the forensic model's assumed 12,691 hidden-positive count are accepted, the implied mean credit lost per deleted dot is about 0.000961 for D2.8→R1 and 0.005029 for R1→B2, below the model's respective break-even thresholds (about 0.054852 and 0.057261). The conditional inversion also estimates G36's 1,200 extra dots at about 0.013422 credit per dot, below its conditional 0.058829 threshold.

These are **conditional algebraic inferences**, not hidden-label measurements or causal proof. A near-catalogue pixel could instead be a missed splay, a registration error, or a real fault. The hide-and-recover proxy does not test the competition's B2 catalogue-pruning premise. Therefore B2's pruning pattern explains how 0.2778 is mathematically plausible; it does not prove why the organizer awarded that score. **A genuinely better new result is possible in principle if it adds high-credit fault coverage without low-value false-positive mass, but no current candidate demonstrates that; no calibrated live-score projection exists.**

The competing overall-leader claims **0.4413** and **0.3195** remain unverified and are not used as baselines.
