## Why GEMSDOE32 B2 has the highest supplied focus-site score

### What the byte-level lineage shows (COMPUTED)

The 44,090-pixel GEMSDOE25/30 D2.8 raster is a parent of the 40,199-pixel H27-4 raster (GEMSDOE28/31 lineage); the latter is a parent of GEMSDOE32 B2 at 37,654 pixels. The audited transitions remove 3,891 and then 2,545 pixels. The removed pixels lie within 1 px and 2 px, respectively, of the mirrored labelled catalogue; B2 has no emitted dots within 2 px of that catalogue. The full receipt is `evidence/nine_site_top_files_2026-10-28.json`; the detailed lineage is `evidence/b2_lineage_recheck_2026-10-29.json`.

The progression of supplied site scores is 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 (**all OWNER-REPORTED**). It is a useful empirical association, but not an independently confirmed organizer experiment and not evidence that pruning caused the score increase.

### Mechanistic explanation (INFERENCE; not causal proof)

The metric is distance-tolerant and asymmetric. A positive prediction near a hidden target can receive partial credit, while a prediction with no nearby target increases the false-positive term. Selectively removing a catalogue-adjacent shell can therefore improve the precision/recall tradeoff if that shell has less hidden-target credit per pixel than the false-positive cost it incurs. It can also reduce redundant coverage and prediction density. The B2 lineage is consistent with this explanation.

Important competing explanations remain: (1) count reduction / generic thinning rather than a special 200 m buffer effect; (2) dependence on hidden labels that are not present in the mirror; (3) different artifact or evaluation conditions among historical submissions; and (4) owner score transcription or lineage errors not visible in public mirrors. A density-matched thinning control has not established the causal mechanism. In particular, the owner-mirror labels are byte-identical to the owner-mirror `existing_faults.tif`; until the official input identity is resolved, the local hide-and-recover target is not certified.

**Can we beat 0.2778?** It is scientifically possible in principle, but it is **not demonstrated** here. No calibrated relationship converts local holdout DTI into an organizer score; the repository records a severe proxy/live scale mismatch. There is no defensible numeric score projection for a new raster. The right decision is not to spend a weekly slot on a candidate whose holdout is compromised or whose lane is undefined.
