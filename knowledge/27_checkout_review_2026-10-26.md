### Why GEMSDOE32 is top among the named focus-site claims

The local mirrored-raster evidence identifies a nested emission/pruning sequence: H19-5 → D2.8 → R1 → B2. In the supplied forensic table, D2.8 has 44,090 emitted pixels, R1 40,199, and B2 37,654. Pixel-set comparison attributes R1 to removing 3,891 D2.8 points within 1 px of mapped faults, then B2 removes a further 2,545 points in the 1–2 px shell. This is a **COMPUTED pixel identity** from mirrors. The claimed scores (0.2600, 0.2708, 0.2778) are **OWNER-REPORTED**. Conditional on those score claims and the official metric form implemented locally, reducing low-credit false-positive mass can raise a Tversky-style score without improving fault detection. This gives a plausible mathematical explanation for B2's increase, not proof of the competition's hidden-label mechanism or actual score.

Important limitation: hide-and-recover masks visible catalogue faults and evaluates hidden catalogue segments. It is a useful spatially blocked proxy, but cannot establish whether a proposed flank-pruning rule improves performance against the competition's unavailable, independent expert truth. Consequently, this review cannot claim that a new submission would exceed 0.2778. User-provided 0.3195 and 0.4413 leaderboard figures conflict across the historical record; neither is an organizer-confirmed receipt in this checkout.
